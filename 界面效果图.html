Text 2

It is a truth almost universally acknowledged that the modern workplace rewards visibility. Yet a growing body of research suggests that the most conscientious employees are often the least recognised, precisely because their contributions prevent problems rather than solve them.

主旨!Economists call this the paradox of prevention: when a crisis is averted, there is nothing to point to. Managers, under pressure to justify budgets, tend to reward the dramatic rescue over the quiet maintenance that made the rescue unnecessary.

Some firms have begun to experiment with alternatives. Rather than annual reviews built around headline achievements, they ask teams to document near-misses and the routines that caught them in time.

26. According to Paragraph 2, the “paradox of prevention” refers to the fact that
[A] crises are more frequent in well-managed firms.
[B] successful prevention leaves little visible evidence.
[C] managers deliberately overlook routine work.
[D] budgets are rarely linked to actual performance.
